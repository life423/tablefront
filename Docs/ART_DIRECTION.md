# Tablefront art direction

Painted miniature military pieces on a recessed flocked campaign board, surrounded by walnut and aged brass. The 0.4.0 visual direction is a detailed handcrafted military diorama: manufactured edges catch the studio light, enamel factions remain readable, and broadleaf trees, slate and planted grass belong to the same physical tabletop language. "Premium tabletop" names this direction rather than claiming finished commercial art.

Shared palette: ink `#172326`, walnut `#3D2A23`, flock `#829071`, route sand `#C0B491`, ivory `#F6EED8`, brass `#DDBB73`. Three deliberate faction enamel accents: blue `#54BAE4`, orange `#EEA05A`, violet `#BD92DF`. UI text colors, material colors and lighting originate in `TablePalette`.

Warm studio light arrives diagonally at 42 degrees with a cool fill and gradient ambient bounce. A restrained ACES grade, bloom, vignette and color adjustment preserve battlefield clarity. A scripted real-time reflection probe captures the table and reflection-only warm/cool softbox cards. Those cards are hidden from the playing camera and give enamel, metal and brass broad highlights. `QualitySettings.realtimeReflectionProbes` is explicitly enabled because its previous disabled value prevented a capture texture from being produced. This is standard URP lighting and reflection capture, without ray tracing or baked global illumination.

Units use purpose-built shared meshes with sloped/chamfered armor, turret and track details, separate recon wheels, and two articulated armored infantry figures. Headquarters add a beveled structure, fittings and communications dish; beacons retain physical pennants, and mechanical rifle-cover locations use detailed sandbags. Shared enamel bases, rings and distinct silhouettes keep class and ownership visible without relying only on color. Limb, knee, wheel and cannon motion is cosmetic; reduced motion holds those poses steady while the actual simulation continues.

The ground is one continuous 50-by-50 mesh with less than 0.04 units of cosmetic relief and low, flat areas around landmarks. Palette-derived color, normal and surface-mask textures blend green and dry flock, irregular earth transitions, six worn supply routes and wheel ruts. Curved grass tufts cluster around groves, outcrops and road shoulders. There are no new decorative colliders or movement obstacles. The route-mask fix uses normalized smooth thresholds; patches and roads are part of the surface rather than separate flat oval geometry.

Enamel, rubber, walnut, brass and stone each have shared generated color, normal and metallic/smoothness maps. Model meshes carry UVs, normals and tangents. All art is generated by the project's C# helpers at runtime; it uses no downloaded models, external texture packs, authored skeletal animation files, paid assets or new packages. A later authored-scene workflow would be needed for baked lighting.

The static scenery is combined by material (14 visible static submissions in the current verification sample). Unit and landmark models retain shared meshes/materials; combat traces and impacts use the existing fixed 128-slot pool. Generated mesh/material/texture ownership remained bounded across the three verified battle/tutorial restart cycles. These checks do not establish a long-run memory limit or measured native frame rate. Generated audio passes through Unity's master listener gain, controlled by the game's saved volume preference; physical listening is a human validation follow-up.

Craft references read: art-direction, scene-lighting, placeholder-assets, character motion, VFX, game-audio and visual scorecard. Unity 6000.6 documentation was studied for URP materials, reflection probes, ambient occlusion, terrain, animation, baking and pipeline trade-offs; installed URP 17.6 source supplies the version-matched Volume API. Resources material references retain the `_NORMALMAP`, `_METALLICSPECGLOSSMAP` and `_OCCLUSIONMAP` surface variants for the player. See [the 0.4.0 visual update](PREMIUM_TABLETOP.md) for captures, verification and remaining limits. AI training and CudaRL integration remain paused.
